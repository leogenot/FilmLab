# Negative density above the published charts

Kodak's stock technical sheets give characteristic curves over a finite log-exposure interval. FilmLab uses approximate readings of those curves for Portra 400, Ektar 100, Gold 200, Tri-X 400, and T-Max 100 (see `STOCK-RESEARCH.md` and the density CSV files). Before this change, FilmLab extended each last chart tangent indefinitely. At very high input light or positive Shot Exposure, the resulting negative density could grow without bound and turn bright detail unnaturally dark in the positive stage.

New grades continue the last chart point with `D = D_end + m_end × 0.75 × (1 − exp(−ΔlogH / 0.75))`. This preserves the endpoint density and first derivative, stays monotone for the positive chart slopes, and approaches a finite additional density. The 0.75 log-exposure scale is an engineering assumption; the Kodak sheets do not establish the actual far shoulder or a scanner's response there. Each RGB layer uses its own last measured slope. The two monochrome studies use their single visual-density curve. Old saved grades keep their linear continuation until the user selects **Use bounded negative shoulder** in Film. The upgrade is undoable and is carried in copied settings and looks.

The renderer probe verifies exact agreement at the chart endpoint, slope continuity there, monotonically increasing density, and a lower density than the prior linear extrapolation two log-exposure units beyond the published range for every stock. This constrains a plausible response outside public measurements; it does not calibrate the film, development, print, or scan transform.
